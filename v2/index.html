01 — RetroRate VHS · 2025–26

A Chrome extension that finds hidden 3% mortgages on Zillow.

Assumable loans are invisible on every major listing portal. VHS flags them while you browse Zillow, Redfin, Realtor.com and Compass, and shows the rate and what you'd save, across all 50 states. Owned end to end, live on the Chrome Web Store.

Full story

Assumable mortgages, FHA and VA loans a buyer can take over at the seller's original rate, look identical to everything else on the market. VHS surfaces them in place, with the monthly and lifetime interest savings against today's rates.

As the extension scaled to more traffic and more portals, calling a preview endpoint for every listing became a bottleneck, and fragile, since a portal changing its page could break it outright. I rebuilt it to read each portal's own listing data as it loaded: faster, and resilient to redesigns that used to take it down overnight. From there I took it national, solved the throughput and race-condition problems of checking thousands of listings at once without hammering our backend, and built the internal dashboard we used to see how people actually used it.

Chrome Web StoreProduct page

TypeScript · React · Vite · Chrome Extensions (MV3) · Node

02 — Loan verification · 2026

AI that reads mortgage statements, and a rules engine that keeps it honest.

Agents upload a statement, the terms get extracted and cross-checked, and a public, confidence-scored certificate comes out the other end. It became a paid feature and a new revenue line.

Full story

Verifying that a listing's loan is actually assumable, and at what rate, used to mean trusting whatever the seller's agent claimed. Gemini and Vertex AI extract the terms and reconcile them against a county-recorder cross-check, with anomaly flags for anything that doesn't line up. The certificate is shareable by QR code and expires automatically when a listing sells or goes off-market.

The real challenge was trusting an AI pipeline enough to stand behind its output publicly, which meant building a rules-based confidence engine and a full audit trail instead of taking the model's answer at face value.

TypeScript · Node · Gemini · Vertex AI · GCP · Stripe

04 — Agent dashboard · 2025–26

Turning a listing classifier into a paid agent product.

A model that spots assumable-loan mentions in MLS remarks became the seed of a dashboard agents pay for, and a second revenue line.

Full story

Most MLS listings never mention that their loan is assumable; agents don't know to say it, or don't know it matters. I built a model that reads MLS remarks and flags likely mentions, then used that signal to promote and rank those listings. That grew into a dashboard where agents see how their listings perform, claim and verify them, and see the savings they're marketing to buyers.

Wiring agent identity to MLS IDs so the dashboard showed the right listings, and building the org-level upsell that turned one agent's usage into a brokerage-wide sale, were what turned a detection feature into revenue.

TypeScript · Node · React · Stripe · MongoDB
